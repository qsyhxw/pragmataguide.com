What Ties These Pages Together

The broad official setup is stable: humans developed Lunafilament, lost contact with the lunar research station dedicated to it, and sent a response team that included Hugh. A quake leaves him stranded, and Diana rescues him. From there, the narrative becomes both a survival run through the Cradle and a relationship story built around how the two characters rely on each other.

Mechanically, the same pairing drives everything. Diana opens enemies and systems through hacking, while Hugh uses weapons and his suit's thrusters to fight and traverse the station. That is why the story pages and the gameplay pages naturally overlap more than they do in most action games.

Best one-line reading:
Pragmata's story is a moonbase mystery built on a two-character partnership, with Lunafilament and the Cradle providing the bigger world around it.

// Shelter

Why The Shelter Matters To Story Readers

Official launch-period coverage treats the Shelter as more than a systems hub. It is where you heal, upgrade, and spend blue Lunafilament, but it is also where Hugh and Diana's bond becomes visible through conversations, Earth memories, and quieter interactions.

That is useful for story readers because it explains why pages like REMs and Diana Synchronization belong close to the narrative cluster. They are not only completion or mechanics pages. They deepen the emotional side of the same story the main lore pages are trying to explain.

Best takeaway:
If the Cradle explains the danger, the Shelter explains the relationship.
